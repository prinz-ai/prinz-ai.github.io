Desportes to Aldobrandini

A warning to Rome, hidden in plain sight.

Desportes explains his silence, attacks failed promises of support and defends Mayenne. Ordinary handwriting gives way to cipher—and returns whenever the writer chooses.

Inside the decipherment

The key was only the beginning.

A known alphabet opened the door. Other letters, old dictionaries, Montaigne—and a few very good human eyes—helped us walk through it. Pick a discovery to see the evidence.

01 / ALPHABET

Start with the pairs.

Tomokiyo’s reconstructed key gives two possible letters for each ordinary sign. The manuscript fixes the shape; language selects the reading.

02 / HANDWRITING

Compare like with like.

Signs in the same hand and contemporary readable copies help distinguish a code, an abbreviation, a connector and a deletion.

03 / LANGUAGE

Read in its own century.

Cotgrave and Nicot preserve lost vocabulary. Montaigne, Amyot, Rabelais and Henry IV’s letters provide grammatical and idiomatic comparisons.

04 / JUDGMENT

Keep the ink and the inference.

An accepted word can include an editorial repair. Historical context can identify a person without proving the value of an unattested sign.
